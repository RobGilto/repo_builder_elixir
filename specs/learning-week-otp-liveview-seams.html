Purpose

A one-week (Jul 6–12), 1–2 hours/day deliberate-practice plan for Robert to close the five understanding gaps triangulated from analyzing all ~150 specs in this repo's history. The repo itself is the textbook: every concept is studied against the real modules, and every exercise is drawn from bugs Robert already shipped and fixed (or hasn't fixed yet). The goal is not more features — it is that by Sunday, Robert can explain why each recurring bug class happened and can design the fix pattern unprompted. This directly answers the "should I rebuild?" question with: no — grow the understanding, keep the repo.

Problem

Three weeks of spec-driven AI development produced a sophisticated platform (orchestrator, worker fleet, dual ADW runtimes, cost guardrails) sitting on top of five recurring gap classes, ranked by pain: (1) OTP process lifecycle & state-of-truth — six variants of "status says running, process is dead" because a terminal event was assumed universal; (2) Phoenix LiveView/Ecto idioms — no live_session until Jul 5, N+1 rollups, eager mounts, reconnect paths repeatedly forgotten; (3) seam/contract design — five parallel launch paths hand-wiring the same options, drifting one sibling at a time, plus hand-maintained Elixir↔Python↔TS registries breaking parity; (4) failure-mode engineering — silent fallbacks (empty prompts, scratch cwd, dropped error reasons) and lifecycles built to start but not end; (5) process hygiene — uncommitted multi-feature trees, duplicate specs, tolerated tooling bugs. The developer's own Jul 4 audit named most of this; what's missing is the underlying mental models and habits so the generator of these bugs stops running.

Solution

Seven daily phases, one focus area every ~1.5 days, each day shaped as Concept (≈25 min) → Trace (≈40 min) → Exercise (≈30 min). Concepts are studied with /teach-me (micro-chunk tutoring) and sketch-diagram (Mermaid whiteboards). Traces read the actual modules and the git commits of fixes already made — including the Jul 5 perf commits (4fc3db4, 5c5daac: 480ms→81ms) which are now study material. Exercises are hands-on: kill processes in IEx and watch the reaper, write one reconciliation test, hunt one N+1, hand-author the LaunchRequest contract design (AI as reviewer only, never author), convert one silent fallback to fail-loud, and close the week with spec triage + a lessons ledger. Every phase ends with explicit validation: a runnable command and/or a "can explain X unaided" quiz via /check-my-understanding.

Notes

  • AI is tutor and reviewer, never author, for the exercises. Concept study uses /teach-me and sketch-diagram freely; but the Day 2 test, Day 5 design doc, and Day 6 conversion are written by hand first, reviewed after. Struggling is the mechanism.

  • Time-box hard. If a day blows past 2 hours, stop and carry the remainder — the daily loop matters more than any single item. Days are independent enough that a missed day shifts, not breaks, the week.

Why these five areas, in this order

This plan operationalizes the spec-archaeology of all ~150 specs (Jun 15 – Jul 5). Ranking is by bug-count × cost: the stuck-status/OTP family shipped ≥6 times and burned real hours and API spend; LiveView reconnect asymmetry shipped ≥5 times; sibling-path drift ≥5 times (and again on Jul 5, after the audit); silent fallbacks caused the most expensive single incident (hours of 429-wall re-dispatch); hygiene amplifies everything else. Notably, the Jul 5 perf work (480ms→81ms once telemetry existed) proved the gap is measurement habit and framework idiom, not ability.
